On this index

The Apparatus

A treatise becomes a reference only when it can be consulted, and a reference is consulted through its index. What follows is the apparatus of the whole work: two indexes, of the figures and of the subjects, each entry pointing to the plates where the figure appears or the idea is developed, and each location a link that opens the plate itself.

The Index of Figures gathers the economists, philosophers, and theorists whose work the volumes draw upon — 138 names, from Acemoglu to Williamson — each followed by the volumes and plates in which they are discussed. The Index of Subjects gathers 49 of the central ideas — from accelerator to transaction costs — each located in the plate or plates that treat it most directly. Together they are the gazetteer of the map: the way to find, among three hundred and twelve plates, the one you want.

Names are listed by surname and ideas by their common term, both alphabetically; the small italic gives the volume, and the figures after it the plates within it, each a link to the plate. The index is selective rather than exhaustive — it records where a figure or idea is treated, not every passing mention — and, like everything in the atlas, it may be entered at any point. To consult it is to step sideways into the work, at exactly the plate one is looking for.

Numéraire The Index · Figures & Subjects

The Index to Numéraire, A Periodic Table of Economic Life — the back matter of the complete work. An Index of Figures of 138 economists, philosophers, and theorists, and an Index of Subjects of 49 central ideas, together carrying more than 550 links into the three hundred and twelve plates of the twenty-six volumes. It is selective, recording where each figure and idea is treated rather than every mention, and it is the apparatus by which the atlas becomes a thing to consult as well as to read. A companion to the atlas, its preface, its guided tour, its laboratory, its constellation, its properties, its compounds, its diagonals, its objections, its chronology, its measures, its cases, its priceless, its gaps, its first person, its schools, its sources, and its unsettled. Set in Fraunces and Newsreader. The argument remains open.
