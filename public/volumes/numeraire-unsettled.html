On the contests of the present

The Verdicts Not Yet In

The sources closed by leaving the reader not at an ending but before a literature still in progress — a conversation whose verdicts are still being handed down, and still open to appeal. This companion shows the verdicts most open of all. They are the contests the table names, being re-fought right now, in our own moment, by forces the old economists never lived to see: the wage met by a machine that thinks, the commons met by a warming world, money met by code that needs no state to vouch for it.

What follows is not prophecy. The book does not forecast; it has no more idea than anyone how these will end, and it distrusts those who claim to. What it offers instead is what it has offered throughout — the contest laid bare: for each, an element of the table reopened by a new force, and the genuine argument on both sides of a question whose answer is not yet settled. The wage against the machine has two centuries of history and two honest readings of it. The commons against the warming world has the table’s own remedy on one side and the table’s own doubt on the other.

These are the places where the book’s long refrain stops being a figure of speech. To say the argument remains open is one thing on a settled page and another thing entirely here, where the verdict has truly not come down, and where the reader is not a student of an old dispute but a party to a live one. Choose a force below to see the contest it reopens. The verdict, in every case, is marked the same: open.

The forces of our moment

The Wage

and the machine that may do the work

For two centuries the machine has been said to be coming for the wage, and for two centuries the wage has survived by moving — the weaver’s loom destroyed a trade and the factory made ten others. Now the machine reaches past muscle into judgment, and the old question returns with a new edge: whether this wave, like the others, will destroy particular jobs while creating more than it takes, or whether a machine that can think is different in kind, displacing faster than the displaced can be absorbed. Both have been argued by serious people; both have history on their side; and history does not yet say which history this will be.

Open

The verdict is not in. It is being fought, right now, in every firm weighing whether the next task comes cheaper from a person or a program.

The Commons

and the warming of the only world

The atmosphere is the largest commons there has ever been, and the hardest: no fence can divide it, no court holds jurisdiction over all of it, and the cost of its use falls heaviest on people not yet born. One side holds that the usual instrument will serve — put a price on the harm, as the table’s own externality prescribes, and let the market find the cheapest path to less of it. The other holds that the scale defeats the instrument: that a commons this global, this slow, and this total asks for something the price alone has never delivered, and that to wait for the market to act is to lose the thing while pricing it.

Open

The verdict is not in. It is the largest open contest the table contains, and the one whose closing the table can least afford to get wrong.

The Money

and its dissolution into code

Money has always been, in the end, a promise the state stood behind. Now it can be issued as code — by no state, by anyone, by a program that pays out on a condition — and the old question of what governs money is suddenly live again. One side sees liberation: a money beyond the reach of the central bank that would debase it, decentralized and free. Another sees a new leash: a money that is programmable is a money that can be watched, frozen, and steered, and a state that issues its own digital coin gains a window onto every payment ever made.

Open

The verdict is not in. What money will be in a generation — whose promise, under whose eye — is being written now, in code and in law at once.

The Firm

and the platform that hollows it

Coase explained the firm as an island of planning, worth its cost wherever directing labor beat buying it piece by piece on the market. The platform redraws the island’s shore: it directs labor minutely — the route, the rate, the rating — while disclaiming the employment that such direction once implied, holding the worker as close as an employee and as far as a stranger. One side calls this freedom: work without a boss, a flexibility the old firm never offered. The other calls it the old control without the old obligation — the direction of an employer, the security of none.

Open

The verdict is not in. What a job is — where the firm ends and the market begins — is being re-drawn in every app that dispatches a car or a meal.

The Power

and the few who hold the network

The table’s power was the power to set the terms others can only take; the network adds a new spring of it, for a thing grows more useful the more who use it, and so the largest tends to swallow the field. One side holds this benign, even efficient: the winner won by being best, the service is free or nearly so, and a better rival is always one click away. The other holds that a handful of networks now sit astride the commerce, the speech, and the attention of the world, with a reach no antitrust law was written to measure — and that free at the price has never meant free.

Open

The verdict is not in. Whether the network is the consumer’s friend or the age’s great unaccountable power is argued in every hearing room that has tried, and failed, to name it.

The Asymmetry

and the data that knows you

Akerlof’s asymmetry was a local thing — the seller knew the car, the buyer did not. The data age has made it vast and one-directional: the platform knows your sleep, your spending, your hesitations, the words you typed and then deleted; you know almost nothing of it in return. One side holds this the fair price of a free service, the knowledge used only to serve you better. The other holds that an imbalance of knowledge this total is an imbalance of power — that to be known so completely by one who is not known at all is a new human condition, and that no click of consent was ever truly informed.

Open

The verdict is not in. Who may know what about whom, and what that knowing is worth, is among the defining contests of the age, and barely begun.

The Choice

and the algorithm that anticipates it

The standard model gave choice to a sovereign chooser, ranking the options by his own lights. The algorithm unsettles the sovereignty: the options are now arranged, ranked, and timed by a system trained on millions like you, to predict and to steer what you will do next. One side calls this service — the shelf tidied to your taste, the search that seems to read your mind. The other calls it a soft hand on the scale: a choice still yours in form, but shaped before you reach it by an intelligence that profits from the shaping, and has learned your weaknesses better than you know them yourself.

Open

The verdict is not in. Whether a choice arranged by another, who gains from the arrangement, is fully a choice is a question as old as persuasion and as new as the feed.

The Share

and the wealth that compounds past merit

The table’s share was your position in the distribution; the question our moment sharpens is whether that position is increasingly inherited. When the return on owning outpaces the growth of earning — when capital compounds faster than wages rise — wealth concentrates, and, handed down, can entrench an advantage that has nothing to do with the merit the system claims to reward. One side holds that returns to capital are earned, that they fund the investment all prosperity rests upon, and that mobility endures. The other holds that compounding inheritance is quietly rebuilding the hereditary order the modern economy was supposed to have dissolved.

Open

The verdict is not in. Whether the coming century belongs to the earner or the heir is fought in every debate over what, and whether, to tax a fortune at death.

The Growth

and the planet that may not sustain it

Growth has been the modern answer to nearly every ill: not how to divide the pie but how to enlarge it, until there was enough for all. The finite planet puts the answer in question. One side holds that growth is the engine of every gain in health and freedom the world has known, and that it can be decoupled from its harms — cleaner, lighter, greener growth without end. The other holds that endless growth on a bounded planet is a contradiction the arithmetic will in time collect on, and that the task is no longer more but enough, fairly shared.

Open

The verdict is not in. Whether prosperity can be had without growth, or growth without ruin, may be the deepest open question the table points to — because every other contest is fought inside its answer.

What the frontier teaches

Contests, Not Destinies

Every age believes its contests are new, and every age is half right. The wage against the machine is two centuries old and freshly sharp; the commons against those who use it is older still; the quarrel over the heir and the earner was old when Ricardo wrote. What our own moment adds is force, and speed, and scale — a machine that reaches into judgment, a commons that is the whole sky, a network that holds the world. The forms are ancient. The pressure is new.

But the deepest thing to see in them is the thing the whole book has been for. These are contests, not destinies. The automation of the wage, the warming of the commons, the hollowing of the firm — each arrives wearing the face of the inevitable, a tide that simply comes in, a future that simply happens. The table was built to strip that face away: to insist that a thing decided by a contest has sides, and stakes, and an outcome that is not yet written, and that what looks like fate is very often only a verdict not yet appealed. To see the largest forces of the age as contests is to remember that they can go more than one way, and that which way is, in part, chosen.

So the book ends where it began, only louder. It opened by insisting that no economic quantity is a natural fact — that the smallest, oldest price is a verdict on a hidden contest, and might have come down otherwise. It closes by insisting the same of the largest, newest questions our moment can pose, which wear the disguise of destiny most convincingly of all and are contests all the same. The table was a training in one kind of listening: to hear, in the warming of the world or the thinking of the machine, the same open structure it first taught you to hear in a wage. The argument remains open. You now know where, in your own time, it is open most — and that you are not its student but its party.

The old verdicts are on the table; the new ones are still out. The argument remains open.
