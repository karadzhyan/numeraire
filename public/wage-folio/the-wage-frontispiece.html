The Argument

What is a wage? This folio answers the question sixteen times over. Each of the first fifteen plates takes the wage up from a single side — as a moral fact, a price for time, a pledge, a measure, a master, an insurance, and ten things more — and finds in it a different essence, every one of them true. The sixteenth gathers the others and argues that the wage has no single essence at all: it is the one number at which a human life and the economy that pays for it are made to meet, and so it is moral and mechanical at once, free and compelled, personal and systemic, irreducibly all of these. The plates were struck in the order set down here, but each stands alone and may be read in any. Together they are an anatomy of the most ordinary number in a working life — the price of an hour, and so of a portion of a person.

Numéraire The Wage · the folio

The Wage — a folio in sixteen plates on the economics and history of the wage: fifteen readings of a single number, and the binding that gathers them. The first leaf weighs the wage’s justice; the last shows why the argument it opens can never quite be closed. Between them lie its hours and its trust, its necessity and its amount, its boundary and its rigidity, its measure and its authority, its risk and its rivalry with the machine, its meaning and its demand, the leisure it might buy back and the schooling that divides one wage from another. Each plate carries its own sources in the margin, set in rubric after the manner of the old printed page. Composed in Fraunces and Newsreader. The plates may be read in any order; this page gives the order in which they were struck, and the argument that binds them. Here the folio begins.
