A Periodic Table
of Economic Life

Every ordinary price — the wage, the rent, the rate — is a verdict on a hidden contest, commonly mistaken for a fact of nature. These are the elements: twenty-six primitives of economic life, each the subject of a volume, each to be turned inside out with the rigor of the seminar and the grace of the essay. The work is to fix a numéraire of honesty against which the verdicts can finally be read. Twenty-three volumes are now in print, and four of the table's six families are complete — the failures, the institutions, the quantities, and the prices. Each family is represented at least twice: The Wage, The Rent, and The Rate, three of the prices; The Margin, The Surplus, The Utility, and The Stock, all four quantities; The Power, The Externality, The Commons, and The Asymmetry, all four failures; The Firm, The Contract, The Money, The Tax, and The Rule, all five institutions; The Cycle and The Growth, the two aggregates; and The Choice, The Game, and The Norm, three of the behaviors.

Numéraire The table of volumes

Twenty-six primitives of economic life, grouped in six families, each to be turned inside out at the seam where the seminar and the essay usually part — with the rigor of the one and the grace of the other, and each scrupulous about the exact edge of what can be known. Twenty-three volumes are now in print, at least two from each of the table's six families — the failures complete at four, the institutions at five, the quantities at four, and the prices complete at five, with three from the behaviors: The Wage (Volume I), whose twelve plates in print run from The Bargain to The Share, and beside it The Rent (Volume II), the return to ownership, opening with The Difference and The Unearned; and beside them among the prices, The Rate (Volume III), the price of time — why a dollar tomorrow is worth less than a dollar today, and who fixes the discount — opening with The Discount and The Usury; The Premium (Volume IV), the price of risk — the cost of bearing what cannot be foreseen, and how much of a fortune is courage and how much mere luck — opening with The Peril and The Fortune; and now, newest of all, The Spread (Volume V), the price of standing between — the cut taken by the third party who joins buyer to seller, and whether he earns it by the value he creates or merely levies a toll on the passage he controls — opening with The Middleman and The Tribute; The Margin (Volume VI), which opens with The Paradox and The Revolution, and beside it among the quantities, The Surplus (Volume VII), the value an exchange creates and the contest over its division, opening with The Gain and The Divide; and, completing the quantities, The Utility (Volume VIII), the want every price is meant to measure and the ghost no instrument can read, opening with The Want and The Ghost; and beside them among the quantities, The Stock (Volume IX), wealth held rather than earned, and the contest beneath a fortune over who holds a claim on what others produce — opening with The Reservoir and The Claim; The Power (Volume X), opening with The Price-Maker and The Single Seller, and beside it The Externality (Volume XI), the cost that escapes the price, opening with The Spillover and The Theorem; and beside them among the failures, The Asymmetry (Volume XII), the breakdown that needs nothing but an inequality of knowledge — the market for lemons and the signal that answers it, the screen and the seal by which trade is unmade and remade by what each party knows — opening with The Lemon and The Signal; and The Commons (Volume XIII), the ruin of what no one owns — long thought a law of nature, and shown by Ostrom to be the absence of rules a community might make — opening with The Tragedy and The Steward; and The Firm (Volume XIV), the island of planning in the priced sea, opening with The Island and The Boundary; The Contract (Volume XV), the law of the deal and the impossibility of writing one that foresees everything, opening with The Gap and The Remainder; The Money (Volume XVI), the volume the table is named for; The Tax (Volume XVII), the burden that slides through prices to settle on whoever can least escape it, and the contest beneath who really pays — opening with The Wedge and The Escape; and The Rule (Volume XVIII), the made background of economic life — property, the law, the enforcing power — on which every price depends, opening with The Frame and The Stakes; The Cycle (Volume XIX), opening with The Glut and The Demand; and beside it among the aggregates, The Growth (Volume XX), the long-run ascent of nations and the institutions that decide it, opening with The Ascent and The Residual; and The Choice (Volume XXIII), opening with The Maximizer and The Taste; and beside it among the behaviors, The Game (Volume XXV), the study of choice grown strategic, where each player's move answers another's, opening with The Move and The Standoff; and The Norm (Volume XXVI), the unwritten rules of conduct — convention mistaken for nature, custom for morality — that govern where no law reaches, opening with The Convention and The Given. The volumes appear as they ripen, not as they are numbered. Set in Fraunces and Newsreader. The argument remains open.
