The argument of the group — and the close of the table

Here the table began, and here it ends. The Prices was the opening family — the wage, the rent, the rate — and it carried, from the first page, the whole argument in miniature: that every ordinary price is a verdict on a hidden contest, commonly mistaken for a fact of nature. Having now passed through the quantities and the failures, the institutions, the aggregates, and the behaviors beneath them all, the argument returns to where it was first made — to be read, this time, whole.

What sets these five apart is that none is quite the price of a thing. The wage is the price of an hour of a life; the rent, of a position others must pass through; the rate, of time itself; the premium, of bearing what cannot be foreseen; the spread, of merely standing between. They are the prices of places in the structure of exchange — and each, looked at closely, rides the same unsettled seam. Is the wage the measure of a worker's worth, or of an employer's power? Is the rent earned by improving the land, or extracted from owning it? Is the rate the reward for patience, or for the control of money? Is the great fortune the wage of courage, or the residue of luck? Does the middleman's cut buy a service, or levy a toll? In every case the orthodox answer — that the price measures a real contribution — meets a heterodox shadow: that it measures, instead, a position held.

That seam, between the earned and the extracted, is the hidden contest the whole table was built to read honestly — and it runs, undisguised, through all five of these everyday verdicts. It is why the work opened here, with The Wage: the price most of us meet first, and argue over longest, and the one volume carried into print. A numéraire of honesty, sought across twenty-six volumes and six families, comes at last to rest on something plain — a way of seeing these five prices for what they are. Not facts of nature. Not the impartial verdicts of a market that merely is. But the outcomes of contests, some fair and some not, that the smoothness of a quoted price was always built to conceal. The circle closes where it opened; the table is whole; the argument, as it was on the first page, remains open.

Group IThe Prices
Numéraire Group I · The Prices

The first of the table's six families — and the last to be set. The Wage, The Rent, The Rate, The Premium, The Spread: the five fundamental prices, each the price not of a thing but of a position, and each riding the same unsettled seam between the earned and the extracted. With this group the table of twenty-six is complete in plan — six families, twenty-six volumes, three hundred and twelve plates conceived; Volume I, The Wage, Volume II, The Rent, Volume III, The Rate, the price of time, Volume IV, The Premium, the price of risk, and Volume V, The Spread, the price of standing between, each now complete in all twelve of its plates — the first five volumes of the table published in full, sixty plates set down in whole, and with them the first of the table’s six families, The Prices, entire. The work opened with the wage and returns to it here; the numéraire it set out to find was, in the end, a way of reading every such price for the contest it conceals. Set in Fraunces and Newsreader. The argument, as ever, remains open.
