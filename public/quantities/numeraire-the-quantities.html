The argument of the group

The first group weighed the prices and found each to be a verdict — on a contest, a power, a history — rather than a fact of nature. This group turns from the verdicts to the magnitudes they are verdicts about: the quantities a market is forever trying to count. There are four of them. The value of one more. The value an exchange leaves on the table. The want a price is built to register. And the wealth that is held rather than earned.

What orders the volumes is an awkwardness they share. Each of the four is foundational, and each is, in its own way, unmeasurable. The margin governs every price, yet may be a calculation no living chooser ever performs. The surplus is real, yet cannot be added across two people without smuggling in a common unit that does not exist. Utility is the thing the whole apparatus is built to track, and the one thing no instrument has ever read. And capital — the most solid-seeming quantity of all — turned out, under a decade of argument between two Cambridges, to be perhaps no single quantity at all.

So this is a science of measurement meeting the four things it cannot quite measure. The numéraire these volumes are after is not another figure to add to the accounts but an honest reckoning of how much of the counting is convention, inference, and faith — and exactly where each of the four stops being a measurement and starts being a decision.

Numéraire Group II · The Quantities

The second of the table's six groups, here brought from sketch to structure. Four volumes — The Margin, The Surplus, The Utility, The Stock — each now set in twelve plates, and the first of them, The Margin, now stands complete in all twelve of its plates — the opening volume of this second family set down in whole, and the second, The Surplus, now begun in print, the first seven of its plates set down, the five volumes of the first, The Prices, standing complete before it. The unifying finding is deliberate and uncomfortable: the discipline's four most foundational quantities are precisely the four it cannot cleanly measure — the margin unobserved, the surplus incommensurable, the utility invisible, the capital non-aggregable. Set in Fraunces and Newsreader. The argument remains open.
