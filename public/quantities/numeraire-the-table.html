A Periodic Table
of Economic Life

Every ordinary price — the wage, the rent, the rate — is a verdict on a hidden contest, commonly mistaken for a fact of nature. These are the elements: twenty-six primitives of economic life, each the subject of a volume, each to be turned inside out with the rigor of the seminar and the grace of the essay. The work is to fix a numéraire of honesty against which the verdicts can finally be read. Volume I, The Wage, is in print.

Numéraire The table of volumes

Twenty-six primitives of economic life, grouped in six families, each turned inside out at the seam where the seminar and the essay usually part — with the rigor of the one and the grace of the other. The table is now complete in plan: three hundred and twelve plates across the twenty-six volumes, every tile open to its twelve, and every group to its essay. Volume I, The Wage, Volume II, The Rent, Volume III, The Rate, Volume IV, The Premium, and Volume V, The Spread now stand published in full, all sixty of their plates complete — the first five volumes of the table whole, the wage of every labour, the rent of every position, the price of all waiting, the price of every risk, and the price of standing between, set down at last; and with them the first of the table’s six families, The Prices, complete entire; and Volume VI, The Margin, the first of the second family, The Quantities, now stands complete, all twelve of its plates set in full; and Volume VII, The Surplus, the second volume of this family, is now begun, the first seven of its twelve plates set down. Set in Fraunces and Newsreader. The argument remains open.
