The Elements

A periodic table of the price system. Twenty-six elemental quantities of economic life — the prices, the quantities they move, the ways they fail, the institutions that hold them, the aggregates they sum to, and the human conduct beneath — arranged in descent, from the surface of exchange to its substrate. The Wage, the price of labor, is № I. The filled cell is the volume so far set in type; the lines trace the mechanisms that volumes share.

NuméraireThe Library

The map of the imprint. Each cell is a volume; each volume an elemental quantity of economic life, to be exhausted in a dozen plates as The Wage exhausts the price of labor. The six families descend from the most concrete — the prices one actually pays — through the quantities they move, the ways they fail, the institutions built to hold them, and the aggregates they sum to, down to the human conduct that underwrites the whole. The arrangement is a claim: that these are the irreducible elements, that they combine, and that each deserves the same treatment — the rigor of the seminar and the grace of the essay, honest to the edge of what can be known. The table is notional. The argument for building it is not. Set in Fraunces and Newsreader, after the plates.
